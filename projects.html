Investigation Thrusts

Student-Oriented Research Projects

Our research combines synthetic organic chemistry, material characterization, and analytical electrochemistry. The following conceptual project descriptions illustrate potential directions for prospective PhD students and research interns.

Direction 1 • Optical & Electrochemical Diagnostics

Multifunctional Molecular Probes for Cellular Gasotransmitter & Microbial Imaging

1. Research Problem

Endogenous gasotransmitters such as hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) mediate critical cardiovascular and neurological signaling pathways, while fungal pathogens pose diagnostic detection challenges. Conventional tools often suffer from cross-reactivity and poor cell permeability.

2. Research Objectives

Synthesize novel hybrid fluorescent frameworks (naphthalimide, triazole, fluorene hybrids) capable of dual optical and electrochemical response with minimal background interference.

3. Methodology & Techniques

Multi-step organic synthesis, column chromatography, ¹H/¹³C NMR, UV-Vis absorption, steady-state fluorescence emission titration, and cyclic voltammetry.

4. Potential Student Activities

Executing reaction schemes, isolating pure fluorophores, tracking fluorescence turn-on kinetics upon addition of analyte, and evaluating selectivity profiles against competing biothiols (cysteine, glutathione).

5. Relevant Academic Background

M.Sc. in Chemistry / Organic Chemistry / Chemical Sciences.

Direction 2 • Interfacial Materials & Sensor Physics

Flexible & Wearable Electrochemical Sensors with Nanocarbon Interfaces

1. Research Problem

Conventional electrochemical sensing setups rely on rigid, bulky glass-carbon or noble-metal electrodes. Flexible, wearable sensing necessitates durable, mechanically compliant interfaces with fast electron transfer kinetics.

2. Research Objectives

Engineer composite interfaces combining synthesized organic receptors with carbon nanomaterials (carbon dots, nanocarbon matrices) on flexible substrates to achieve high electrochemical sensitivity.

3. Methodology & Techniques

Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS), Differential Pulse Voltammetry (DPV), Chronoamperometry, surface characterization, and drop-casting fabrication.

4. Potential Student Activities

Fabricating electrode coatings, characterizing charge-transfer resistance across the interface, optimizing analyte calibration curves, and testing sensor performance under mechanical bending.

5. Relevant Academic Background

M.Sc. in Chemistry, Materials Science, Applied Physics, or Nanotechnology.

6. Related Laboratory Publications

• J. Coatings Technol. Res. (2025, DOI: 10.1007/s11998-025-01138-z)
• J. Fluoresc. (2024, DOI: 10.1007/s10895-024-03902-7)

Direction 3 • Environmental & Applied Nanotechnology

Green Nanostructures & Fluorescent Nanomaterials for Catalysis and Monitoring

1. Research Problem

Developing sustainable, environmentally benign synthetic routes for metallic nanostructures and carbon quantum dots for targeted reduction of environmental toxins.

2. Research Objectives

Utilize bio-derived and green reducing agents (e.g., vitamin-C, sophorolipids) to fabricate uniform copper nanowires and functional carbon dots for catalytic remediation.

3. Methodology & Techniques

Hydrothermal synthesis, UV-Vis spectroscopy, SERS activity measurements, catalytic reduction of 4-nitrophenol, and transmission electron microscopy data analysis.

4. Potential Student Activities

Optimizing hydrothermal parameters, evaluating catalytic rate constants, and synthesizing stable fluorescent ink formulations.

5. Relevant Academic Background

Chemistry, Nanoscience, or Materials Chemistry.

6. Related Laboratory Publications
